# Where the watts go: a powertop pass

Written 2026-09-10. The 2026-08-25 note recorded *how much* the machine draws
(median 14 W over a week) and who owns the policy knobs. This one asks *where* the
draw goes, using powertop 2.16 for the per-device and per-process view and the
battery's own `power_now` as ground truth. Nothing on the machine was changed to
produce it, and the candidate list at the end is exactly that: candidates.

## Method

powertop was installed and calibrated (`powertop --calibrate`, ~10 min, on battery,
laptop untouched). Then three 300 s windows, each on battery, each recorded as a
powertop CSV with `/sys/class/power_supply/BAT0/power_now` sampled every 10 s
alongside:

| Window | Condition |
| --- | --- |
| idle | shell up, two kitty windows and a terminal agent spinner, WeChat in the background, hands off |
| typical | Firefox, kitty, editor, WeChat, normal work |
| screen off | as idle, then `hl.dsp.dpms({action="off"})`, hands off |

Three caveats on what powertop could and could not say here:

- **No per-device watt estimates.** powertop only prints those after it has
  collected ~300 discharge measurements of its own (the log says so:
  `To show power estimates do 295 measurement(s)`). One calibration and three
  windows is nowhere near that, so its device report is usage percentages and
  wakeup rates only. The watt numbers below all come from the battery.
- **The C-state and frequency reports are junk on this box.** `cpufreq_stats`
  fails to load, the idle driver is `acpi_idle` with generic C1/C2/C3 names, and
  every core reports "Idle 100%" in the frequency table. Ignored.
- **The screen-off window was dirty for its first 90 s.** Any key or pointer input
  relights the panel, and the run was started by typing. The steady dark portion
  is the last 150 s; the numbers below use that.

## Measured

| Window | Battery draw | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| idle, screen on, 50% backlight | **9.6 W** mean, 9.0 W floor | APU `PPT` reads 4.4 W of it |
| typical | **11.1 W** mean, 7.0–14.6 W | wifi at 104 pkts/s, Firefox 40 wakeups/s |
| screen off, steady | **4.1 W** mean, 4.0 W floor | 4.0–4.2 W for 150 s straight |

The two numbers that matter are 9.6 and 4.1. Turning the panel off drops the
machine by about **5.5 W, or 57% of idle draw**. That is the panel plus everything
the display pipeline keeps awake when there is a scanout (DCN, display clocks,
compositor frames), not the backlight alone, but it is the lever. It is also
consistent with the week-long p10 of 7.5 W in the earlier note: that is roughly
this idle state at a lower brightness.

The 4 W floor with the screen off is the rest of the machine at rest: SoC, RAM,
the NVMe held in PS0 (see below), wifi associated, Bluetooth powered. It is also
what the machine burns for the six minutes between the 300 s lock timeout and the
360 s dpms timeout in `hypridle.conf`, and for every minute of the lock timeout
itself, at 9.6 W.

## Wakeups

~240 wakeups/s in every window, screen on or off. It does not go down when the
display does, which says the sources are software, not display:

| Source | idle | screen off | typical |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `tick_nohz_handler` (scheduler tick, `CONFIG_HZ=1000`) | 117/s | 93/s | 84/s |
| Hyprland | 20/s | 14/s | 12/s |
| kitty ×2 | 15 + 8/s | 12 + 11/s | – |
| terminal agent spinner | 13/s | 10/s | – |
| WeChat (4 Chromium processes) | – | 6 + 5 + 4 + 3/s | – |
| Firefox parent | – | – | 40/s |
| `sched(softirq)` | 15/s | 18/s | 16/s |
| `drm_sched_run_job_work` (GPU jobs) | 6.6/s | 9/s | 4.8/s |

Two things stand out. The GPU is still submitting jobs and `commit_work` still
fires 4/s with the panel dark, which means something keeps rendering frames into
a disabled output: the terminal spinner and WeChat are the obvious suspects. And
the amdgpu interrupt runs at ~240/s with `gpu_busy_percent` at 14% during "idle",
which is a compositor that is never actually idle because two terminals are
animating. Neither is a config bug; both are a reminder that the shell's own
timers (`SysState.qml` 5 s poll, `DetailsMenu.qml` 5 s, `Blink.qml`) are not
what is keeping the CPU awake.

The touchpad's I2C controller (`AMDI0010:03`, IRQ 6) has 2.2 M interrupts since
boot but 0/s when the hands are off. Usage, not a leak.

The `mt792x_pm_wake_work` entry (7 s/s of work time in the typical window) is
the wifi chip leaving 802.11 power save for every burst of traffic. That is the
cost side of wifi power save being on; the saving side is why it stays on.

## Tunables, with verdicts

powertop lists 36 "bad" settings. Most are not.

**Do not touch, documented reasons:**

- `Runtime PM for ... WD PC SN540 / SN350 NVMe` and, by extension, any ASPM
  change. `nvme_core.default_ps_max_latency_us=0` on the kernel command line
  disables APST on purpose; this drive hangs the machine when it is allowed to
  sleep (MACHINE.md, "The NVMe hang"). Runtime D3 is the other half of the same
  crash. The cost is that the SSD idles in PS0 all day, likely 0.5–1 W of the
  4 W floor, and it is the price of a machine that stays up. **This is why
  `powertop --auto-tune` must never be run here**: it applies every entry in the
  list, including this one.
- `Runtime PM for ... MT7921` wifi. Same driver family that already needs its
  wake work; PCI runtime PM on this chip is a known source of stalls, and the
  802.11-level power save is already on and is the part that matters.
- `Intel energy performance preference → balance_performance`. Generic Intel
  advice and wrong for battery; EPP is owned by power-profiles-daemon.

**Noise, no measurable effect:** runtime PM for the Data Fabric functions
`00:18.0–7`, root complex, IOMMU, dummy host bridges, GPP bridge, SMBus, LPC,
PSP; KSM and khugepaged sleep intervals; `dirty_ratio`; `default_qdisc`. The
iGPU `04:00.0` entry is also noise: an APU with an attached panel cannot
runtime-suspend, and the driver does not offer it.

**Real, small, cheap:**

- `panel_power_savings` (AMD ABM) at `/sys/class/drm/card1-eDP-1/amdgpu/`,
  currently `0`. Content-adaptive backlight: dims the backlight and compensates in
  the pixel values. On a panel that costs ~5.5 W this is the one entry with a
  plausible 0.3–1 W behind it. It also visibly changes the image at high levels,
  which is a theme-never-costs-usability question, so it needs to be looked at, not
  just measured.
- `04:00.5` Audio Coprocessor has no driver bound and `power/control = on`. With
  `auto` the PCI core would leave it in D3hot. Free, tiny.
- `04:00.3` / `04:00.4` xHCI runtime PM, blocked by the one USB device that is
  still `on`: `1-3`, the ASUS N-KEY keyboard (`0b05:19b6`, three HID interfaces).
  Keyboards over USB autosuspend are usually fine because they remote-wake, and
  occasionally drop the first keystroke. Needs a day of use to judge.
- Bluetooth is powered with nothing connected. It lives on USB `3-4`, which
  already autosuspends, so the radio is the whole cost: 0.05–0.2 W. Whether it
  should be off by default is a usability call, not a tuning one.
- `snd_hda_intel power_save` is 10 s; powertop wants 1 s. Milliwatts, and 1 s is
  where codec pops come from. Leave it.
- `nmi_watchdog=0`, `sched_autogroup_enabled=0`, `dirty_writeback_centisecs=1500`.
  Harmless, and each below the noise floor of a 10 s battery sample. Writeback
  interval in particular buys nothing while the NVMe never sleeps.

## The 2026-08-25 bug is live right now

At the time of the measurements: `AC0 online` = 0, UPower says `discharging`,
and `powerprofilesctl get` says **balanced**. The rule in `SysState.qml`
(`syncProfile()`, line 380) should have put it in `power-saver` on this
battery-since-boot session and did not, for the reasons the earlier note lays out.
Every window above was therefore taken with `epp=balance_power`, which is the
pessimistic case. The A/B section below shows why it will never be otherwise
until the shell is fixed.

None of the three fixes from that note had landed when this was measured
(they land later the same day, see the end of the A/B section):
`upower.service` and `power-profiles-daemon.service` are both still `disabled`;
there is no udev rule on `power_supply` (only `80-nvidia-runtime-pm.rules`); the
`onBattery` ternary at `SysState.qml:27–31` is unchanged. The argument for them is
not repeated here. The measurable cost of the missing profile switch is the next
thing to establish: five minutes idle in `power-saver` against the 9.6 W above.

Unrelated but noticed: `~/.config/systemd/user/lowbattery.{service,timer}` are
disabled, inactive, and point at a script that no longer exists. Dead files, not a
power problem, not in this repo.

## The profile A/B: 0.2 W, and a shell that forbids power-saver

Two attempts. The first, four interleaved 150 s phases with the screen on, came
out **10.3 W balanced against 13.6 W power-saver** and was not a measurement of
anything. The second, four 300 s phases locked and dark with the profile read
back on every sample and a bus monitor running, produced one clean pair:

| Phase (first 60 s dropped) | Draw | EPP |
| --- | --- | --- |
| balanced | 4.77 W (4.70–4.84) | balance_power |
| power-saver | 4.56 W (4.46–5.10) | power |

**power-saver is worth about 0.2 W at idle with the panel dark**, some 4%, which
is the size of saving you would expect when the APU is already at 4 W. Screen-on
and under load were not measured cleanly. The other power-saver phase, and every
earlier try, was destroyed by the three things below.

**Every profile switch wakes the dGPU.** Setting either profile takes `01:00.0`
from `D3cold` to `D0` for 18–25 s; the battery reads 18–21 W for the next minute
with the APU's own `PPT` flat at 4 W, so the burst is the GeForce. The likely
path is `platform_profile` → asus-wmi → an ACPI notification the NVIDIA driver
answers by powering up. Roughly 0.04 Wh per switch. It also triggers a UPower
refresh 1–2 s later, outside the daemon's 30 s cadence.

**power-saver cannot stay set while the shell is running.** Established in
order, each step ruling out the previous suspect:

1. In every try the active profile went back to `balanced` 20–230 s after being
   set, 26–43 ms after a UPower `PropertiesChanged` that carried a **Percentage**
   change, with no short-lived client on the bus in between. Routine 30 s updates
   without a percentage change did nothing.
2. Not power-profiles-daemon's battery-aware mode: with
   `powerprofilesctl configure-battery-aware --disable` the revert still landed
   on the next percentage tick. (Re-enabled afterwards; `query-battery-aware`
   reads True.) ppd also does not re-apply a saved profile: `state.ini` follows
   every client set.
3. The shell: with `qs` frozen by `SIGSTOP`, `power-saver` survived a
   percentage tick by 20 s. Three seconds after `SIGCONT` the profile was
   `balanced`, the shell having processed the queued update.
4. The deck's own QUIET button: tapping it produced `ActiveProfile =
   power-saver` and, **40 ms later**, `ActiveProfile = balanced`. The user saw
   the row jump back. QUIET is a button that cannot work.

And the reason the shell writes `balanced` is the 2026-08-25 bug, live: the bar
shows `73` with no time-to-empty, which is the `!onBattery` branch of
`batteryText` (`SysState.qml:47`), while UPower's DisplayDevice, BAT0, AC0 and
its daemon flag all say discharging. `syncProfile()` (`SysState.qml:381`) does
exactly what it says with a wrong `onBattery`: `Balanced`, every time it runs.
What makes it run on every percentage tick and 40 ms after every profile change
is not pinned down; the only callers are `Component.onCompleted` and
`onOnBatteryChanged`, so quickshell 0.3.0's UPower binding must be re-emitting a
transition through `state` or `ready` on those updates. That detail changes
nothing about the fix.

The cost is now measurable rather than guessed: the shell has held ppd on
`balanced` for every battery session since at least 2026-08-25, and the deck's
manual override has been a no-op. At 0.2 W idle that is small; what it forbids is
any future policy that relies on `power-saver` at all.

Fixed the same evening, in the order the 2026-08-25 note gave: `upower` and
`power-profiles-daemon` enabled; the AC-to-profile rule moved to
`system/etc/udev/rules.d/85-power-profile-ac.rules` plus a oneshot
`power-profile-ac.service` that also runs at boot; `syncProfile()` and its
callers removed from `SysState.qml`; and the `onBattery` ternary made to fall
through to the daemon flag when `state` is `Unknown`.

Unrelated oddity seen while looking: `cpu0`, `cpu2` and `cpu14` report
`scaling_min_freq = scaling_max_freq = 1102866` (the `amd_pstate_lowest_nonlinear_freq`)
while the other 13 report 4465261. It is cosmetic: pinned to `cpu0` under load,
`scaling_cur_freq` read 3.8 GHz. Nothing in this repo writes those nodes; the
machine has suspended three times this boot, and amd-pstate-epp's resume path is
the usual suspect for stale limit values.

Two footnotes on method: `power_now` on this battery is a filtered value that
updates every 10 s and takes ~60 s to settle after a step, so short phases are
mostly transient; and `hypridle` locks at 300 s idle and darkens at 360 s, so a
hands-off run either starts locked and dark or measures three different states.
Scripts for the locked A/B, the battery-aware test and the freeze test lived in
the session scratchpad; the shape is in the text above.

## Ranked candidates

Ordered by watts per unit of risk. Nothing here has been applied.

1. **Shorten the display timeout on battery.** The panel is 5.5 W and
   `hypridle.conf` waits 360 s to darken it, 60 s after the lock. Darkening at the
   lock, or before it, is the largest saving available and touches one number in
   one file. Cost: none while the machine is locked anyway.
2. **Take profile policy out of the shell.** The A/B section shows the shell
   actively undoing `power-saver` on every battery tick and breaking its own
   QUIET button, so the 2026-08-25 fix list is now a bug fix rather than a
   tidy-up: udev rule on `power_supply`, the two `enable`s, and `syncProfile()`
   either removed or made to read `AC0/online`. Worth 0.2 W at idle and the
   ability to use `power-saver` at all.
3. **Try ABM.** `echo 2 > .../panel_power_savings`, look at it for an hour, read
   `power_now`. If it earns its keep and does not spoil the image, persist it the
   same way `panel-od-off.service` persists overdrive. It is a one-line sysfs write
   in the same class as the existing unit.
4. **Backlight.** Not a config change: the 50% level in the idle window is the
   biggest single number on the sheet, and every step down is ~0.1 W.
5. **A udev rule for the driverless audio coprocessor and the xHCI pair**, plus
   USB autosuspend on the keyboard, under `system/etc/udev/rules.d/`. Perhaps
   0.1–0.3 W combined, with a keyboard to watch for a day.
6. **Bluetooth off when nothing is paired.** ~0.1 W; usability call.
7. **Process hygiene.** WeChat idle at ~18 wakeups/s across four processes and
   Firefox at 40/s are the two biggest user-space sources. Closing what is not in
   use is worth more than any sysctl on the list.

Not candidates: NVMe APST, NVMe runtime PM, ASPM policy, wifi runtime PM, EPP,
`powertop --auto-tune`. See the verdicts above.

## Reproducing

```sh
sudo powertop --time=300 --iteration=1 --csv=out.csv     # on battery, hands off
while :; do cat /sys/class/power_supply/BAT0/power_now; sleep 10; done
```

Remember that any input relights a dpms-off panel; start the dark window from a
command, then keep hands off the whole 300 s. Calibration data lives in
`/var/cache/powertop/saved_parameters.powertop` and survives reboots.
